## Doodle
- Brand identity leans playful/consumer: a bright, saturated palette historically built around teal, coral/magenta and yellow, rounded shapes, a friendly illustrated tone on marketing pages.
- Product UI is a calendar/grid-first layout: options as rows or columns, participants as the other axis, checkmarks/dots as the response glyph, with the "best option" surfaced via a highlighted column and a small badge.
- Reviewers' most common visual complaint: ads and upsell banners embedded in the working area of free-tier pages, which clutters exactly the page an organizer is sharing with outsiders.

## Rallly (open-source poll tool — reference, not a source of code)
- Positions itself as calm and minimal: no ads by design, a neutral light UI, an emphasis on "nothing to sign up for" being visible immediately on the voting page.

## When2meet / Crab Fit (availability-grid tools — reference)
- Deliberately bare-bones, utilitarian; When2meet in particular is often cited (fondly and critically) for having almost no visual design at all. Crab Fit modernizes the same grid idea with a heatmap gradient and clearer typography.

## What this tells the wedge
The category's biggest visual complaint about the incumbent is clutter (ads, upsells) inside the working page, not a lack of features. The differentiated visual move for `rustic-fjord` is not "more polished than Doodle" — it's **quiet**: nothing competing with the grid for attention, generous whitespace, no marketing chrome inside the app itself, and a palette that reads as calm/utilitarian rather than playful-consumer (avoiding Doodle's teal/coral/yellow system) or generic SaaS (avoiding a blue/purple gradient default).
